# Popular, skill-based, high-learning toddler games (research notes)

| Game | Why it's a hit | Skill / learning |
|---|---|---|
| Toca Kitchen / Toca Hair Salon (Toca Boca) | Hugely viral; open-ended "do anything" play | Cause and effect, real-world routines, no fail state |
| Sago Mini World | Charming characters; tactile and calm | Pretend play, routines, sequencing |
| Pok Pok | Calm "digital toy room" in a Montessori style | Open-ended manipulation; low stimulation |
| Busy Shapes / Montessori apps (Edoki) | Award-winning; based on Piaget's theory of how children's thinking develops | Puzzles that adapt to the child; real problem-solving |
| Thinkrolls (Avokiddo) | Big with 3-year-olds and up | Logic and physics puzzles: push, roll, float |
| Endless Alphabet (Originator) | Very popular | Letters and vocabulary through funny animated word monsters |
| Khan Academy Kids | Most recommended free app | Structured curriculum: maths, reading, social-emotional |
| Peekaboo Barn (Night & Day Studios) | Classic first app for ages 1–2 | Animal names and sounds; object permanence |
| Dr. Panda, Bimi Boo, Lingokids, PBS Kids Games | Mass-market hits | Routines, sorting, early maths and language |

## What to borrow
1. **Active over passive.** The child thinks and does: fit, build, choose, transform. Most "educational" apps are low-quality drill (Psychology Today; EdWeek).
2. **Responsive feedback.** Learning transfers best with contingent responses, such as step-by-step demos and spoken cues. Only 15% of apps repeat an instruction after a pause (Frontiers 2021 meta-analysis).
3. **The Toca formula:** open-ended play, no failing, real-world activities, and the child's creation stays.
4. **The Pok Pok and Edoki formula:** calm, few sounds, and puzzles that adapt to the child.

## Ideas this suggests for Little Learners
- Toca-style free play: a hair salon or a doctor's surgery.
- Thinkrolls-style physics puzzles: push, roll and float.
