# Audit: Roll & push (`roll`, Building site world)

Checked against `docs/storyboards/README.md` (the audit list), `docs/FEEL-BRIEF.md`, `docs/SATISFACTION.md`, `docs/QUALITY-BRIEF.md` and the spec `docs/design/roll.md`.
- **Storyboard:** `docs/storyboards/roll.md`.
- **Code spot-checked:** `app/src/lessons/roll.js`, `roll-courses.js`, `roll-pieces.js`, `tools/blender/creatures/prop_roll.py`, and the engine files `app/src/engine/lesson.js`, `speech.js`, `audio.js` (`playVoice`), `input.js`, `fx.js` and `ui.js` (`showGo`).
- **Voice:** checked line by line against `app/assets/voice/index.json` and `tools/voice/new-lines.txt`. Lines that are only waiting for a clip are not counted as design issues.

| Game | Verdict | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Roll & push (`roll`) | **needs work** | The core loop is the best in the world. A baby rolls, stops and looks at the piece; her finger sinks, pushes or presses it; real physics carries the baby home; the nest fills up and stays full. Four things undercut it. (1) The reward is cut off: the camera leaves the nest before Mummy counts, on most puzzles. (2) The help ladder is broken: hand demos lock out her taps and loop forever, and the errorless step never happens. (3) Colour = verb breaks for the jelly, cookie, stone and cart. (4) Levels 2 and 3 are 14 puzzles each and too dense for a 2-year-old. |

What works:
- The stop is very readable: the baby brakes, turns to the piece, then looks at the child.
- Every first-of-kind piece gets a slow ghost-hand demo on the real object.
- Pieces can be used in any order, even while the baby rolls.
- A box drops into its hole under her finger.
- The level plays a rising tune, one note per action.
- There is no wrong answer, and the nest build-up persists across levels (`rollHogs`).
- The table re-forms through the plinth, with no pops or fades.
- Level 1 has a gentle, well-ordered curve.

## Storyboard "broken" list: confirmed or refuted

| 1 | Uh-oh overwritten by the instruction | **Confirmed, and worse** | `onStop` (roll.js 1501–1505) calls `sayLine(UH)` and `sayInstr(line)` in the same frame, so the caption flips at once. ### Answers to the four questions

1. **Is each new piece taught clearly before being combined?** Mostly yes. Cookie (2-5), crate (2-8), jelly (2-12), plank (2-9), cart (2-10), lift (3-1) and balloon (3-5) each appear alone first, with a demo. There are three exceptions:
   - The **stone** is never taught and never named (R8).
   - The **balloon's second action** (pop) is never demoed (R10c).
   - The **box in L3** is first needed in 3-4 but only demoed if she is stuck (R6).
   
   L2 introduces too many new pieces for one level (R7).
2. **Does colour = verb hold everywhere?** No: the green jelly, tan cookie, grey stone and blue non-pushable cart all break it (R4). Doors and lifts follow the rule but are not tappable themselves (R11).
3. **Is the difficulty curve gentle enough?** L1 is: one idea per puzzle, never more than 3 actions. L2 is too wide (seven novelties) and L3 too long (about 10 min, ending on 4- and 5-action courses). The 10/12/12 cut in R7 fixes both. The "Think" goal (ordering) is spoken for her ("Open the door first!"), which is right for age 2; R6 keeps it that way without the abstract "first".
4. **Any lock-outs inside puzzles?** No unwinnable state was found. Every rail range, the hole stop and the door-blocked push were traced on every course, and all of them recover. There are real input lock-outs, though: every demo eats taps for 1.3 s (R2). The help ladder can never reach errorless (R3); and with R3 fixed, the balloon hover would still be a dead end at the errorless step (R10b).
