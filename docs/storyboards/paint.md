# Paint & mix (id: `paint`)

- **World:** Meadow (the app's "Painting!" tile, blue). The scene is a wooden painting table (a few dry paint splats on it) with an easel on a low riser (its board a soft blue-grey, so the pale wooden toy stands out), a gallery wall behind it (out of the picture until the finale) and Pip on the left. The brush rests on the easel ledge's left part; the stamp stands in a small turned cup at the ledge's left end. The meadow ground sits below the table.
- **Learning goal:** make and control marks with a brush (dip, rub, tap, stamp), paint the colour you are asked for, then make the colour you need by mixing two paints in a dish; finished toys are hung on the wall.
- **Levels:** 3 (chosen by the parent; 8 rounds each, 24 in all). L1 free painting, one new idea per round. L2 "paint it the colour asked" (a friend or a picture card shows the answer). L3 mix the colour first (orange, green, purple, pink), then paint.
- Source: `app/src/lessons/paint.js` (rounds are the `L1`/`L2`/`L3` tables at the top; their `free`, `splats`, `orStamps` and `rainbow` numbers are the finish thresholds), `paint-canvas.js` (paint surface, toys, picture cards), `paint-studio.js` (models), `paint-shapes.js` (colours, mix table, recipe lines), `paint-sound.js`; engine `app/src/engine/lesson.js`. Audit: `docs/storyboards/AUDIT-paint.md` (every fix in it is applied; see the last section).

## How to read the tags

- `[voiced]` = the line has a clip in `app/assets/voice/index.json` (checked with `voiceKey`).
- `[pending voice]` = no clip yet; the line is in `tools/voice/new-lines.txt` waiting to be recorded. Until then it is shown as a caption and, when sent with `voice:true`, falls back to the system speech voice. Lines sent with `voice:false` stay silent text.
- Only these lines in the game have a clip today: "Which one?", "One.", "Two.", "Three!", "Bye bye! See you soon!", "You did it! A sticker!". Everything else is `[pending voice]`.
- Voicing rules in the code: in L1 and L2 a colour name ("Red!") is voiced once per colour per level, then caption only; in L3 colour names are caption only (the recipe line names both colours). "More paint!", "Paint it all!", "Hang it up!", "Stamp, stamp, stamp!" and each recipe line are voiced once per level, then caption only. The first wrong-colour line of a step is voiced. Re-prompts are caption only; the engine also drops any line spoken in the last 30 s to caption only.

## Behaviour shared by every round (not repeated in every row)

- **Nothing paints by itself.** Paint lands only exactly under her finger (or the mouse pointer) while she is pressed on the toy and moving, laid at every pointer sample (coalesced events) with a dab every few pixels, so a fast stroke has no gaps; or as one splat where she taps (a touch shorter than 0.4 s that moves less than 0.12 of the short screen side). Touching a pot only dips the brush. Hand demos are a ghost hand only and never leave paint, dip or mix for her. Autoplay (parent's test mode only) paints through the very same stroke path.
- **Paint feel:** a dab is 0.22 world units wide (about 60 to 70 px at the game's framing, wider than a toddler's fingertip, so colour shows on both sides of her finger as she rubs); a splat is 0.32, a stamp dot 0.24. Colours are thick poster paint: red `#e8302a`, yellow `#ffbf00`, blue `#1f66e5`, green `#1fae4b`, pink `#ff6fae`, orange `#ff8000`, white, purple `#8a3fdc`. Speed changes the width a little; a nearly dry brush shows bristle gaps. The burnt lines and the face stay on top. On the butterfly the paint mirrors onto the other wing.
- **Camera and layout (L1, L2):** the easel stands at the back centre on a 0.15 riser; this round's pots stand close in front of it (z 0.35, 0.6 apart, centred); Pip at the left, the friend or picture card at the right (1.6). The camera looks in low (about 14°) and is fitted every round to the board, the toy (where it will hang on the easel peg), the pots, Pip and the friend, with a 32 px margin and the top 13.5 % of the screen kept free for the caption bubble, so the caption never covers the toy or its ring. A toy is scaled up on the easel to the board's height (1.3) or, for a wide toy, nearly the board's width (1.9 of 2.0). Measured toy heights are in the last section. At round start the camera eases 0.7 s to the new fit (she can already act).
- **Camera and layout (L3, two stations):** the easel stands right of centre. The mix station is on the left: the four pots in a row at the back, the mixing dish in front of them (tipped toward her and lifted clear of the table top), a small stack of ten spare dishes (saucer size; a dish grows to full size as it comes to the mixing spot) at the table's left end, and one tidy pile of used dishes behind the stack. The paint view (looking in a little lower, about 10°) frames the easel, the toy, the dish (her paint source) and every open pot; the spare stack and the used pile are out of it. The mix view (a close-up from above) frames the dish, the pots, the spare stack, the used pile and Pip. The camera glides to the mix view at the start of every mixing step and on her first dab into the dish (0.8 s), and back to the paint view 0.2 s after the colour appears (0.9 s).
- **The tools:** the brush comes to her finger when she presses on the toy, a pot, the dish or a saucer (no pick-up step); it lies back on the ledge's left part when she lets go. When she paints near the top of the screen the handle tips down to the right so it stays in the picture. Touching a pot dips it (it glides in, a "plip", a drop falls, the colour name shows); dragging through a pot also dips. Each pot has a rolled rim and a paint drip of its colour down the front. The load runs out after a stroke of about 4 to 6 toy-widths; then "More paint!" and the pot she used bobs (in L3 the dish bobs, because the dish is her paint source). A tap on a lidded pot wobbles the lid (soft "tok"). Tapping the stamp (in its cup) swaps brush and stamp; tapping the brush swaps back. The stamp holds 6 prints per dip.
- **Sounds:** `plip` (dip), `swish` (start of a stroke, at most once per 0.8 s), `pat` (splat; lower for a stamp), `tok` (lid), a very quiet wet `squish` while she stirs (its volume follows her finger's speed; it stops when the colour is made). No boings, no chimes for success.
- **Rule 0 (no waiting):** the next round's toy and pots are set up while the finished toy settles on its peg; her first possible action comes 0.2 to 0.6 s after each round starts (logged as `PAINT_READY` in the console) and about 1.0 to 1.5 s after the last toy was hung. The Go! badge shows once the round has been live 0.3 s; the game does not gate her touches on it.
- **Round end is hers:** once the round's goal is met (below) and she has lifted her finger for 0.6 s (a finger resting still 4 s counts as lifted), a big "hang it up" sign (a framed heart on a little post, about 12 % of the screen high) pops up standing on the easel ledge's right end, or, when the toy reaches down into that corner, in a top corner of the board below the caption, and glows. Its tap target is a ring of about a tenth of the screen round it (a touch on her toy outside the sign itself still paints). The brush lies down at the ledge's left part, clear of it. "Hang it up!" is said (voiced the first time per level) and the `done` hand (tap on the sign) shows once. She can keep painting as long as she likes; the round only ends when she taps the sign. Then the toy squashes once and the gallery hook comes down on its twine cord just above the easel (0.85 above the toy's ring, in front of the board) and the brush lies down at the ledge's left end; the camera stays close and eases up a little, keeping the whole toy, the hook and its cords' knot below the caption (the toy stays big, the hook is less than a third of the screen above it). She drags the toy up onto the hook (or taps the toy or the hook: it lifts itself there in 0.7 s). A drop anywhere else sends it back to the easel peg. On the hook it settles with one small wobble and a rising note; 0.4 s later the cord pulls hook and toy up out of the picture to its peg on the gallery wall, while the next blank toy is already rising behind the board.
- **Goals (read from the round table):** free rounds: 25 % of the toy covered in any colour (`free: 0.25`), or 3 splats in the tapping round (`splats: 3`), or 4 stamp dots in the stamp round (`orStamps: 4`). Directed steps: 60 % of the asked part in the asked colour (counted per colour, so other paint on it does not count). Spots step: 3 counted spots. Rainbow: 3 bands each a third painted (`rainbow: 3`). If she stops early (6 s with some paint and no goal) one gentle line comes, caption-only except the first "Paint it all!": "Paint it all!" in rub rounds, "Tap, tap, tap!" in the tapping round, "Stamp, stamp, stamp!" while she holds the stamp in the stamp round, "Stamp three white spots!" in the spots step.
- **Wall:** eight pegs in two rows of four (top row first), above and behind the easel, out of the picture while she paints (its posts stand at its far ends). The toy of round N goes up to peg N, so the wall fills over the level; she sees it in the finale.
- **Hand demos (ghost hand only):** each action is shown once per play of the level, when first needed: `dip` (tap on the pot, or in L3 on the dish once it holds her colour, 1.8 s), `rub` (zigzag over the toy or the asked part, 2.6 s), `splat` (two taps, 2.4 s), `stamp` (stamp, pot, toy, 2.6 s; in the spots step it ends on a ring mark), `dish` (pot to dish, 2.2 s), `stir` (circle over the dish, 2.6 s), `pick` (a scan across the three toys to choose from, 2.4 s), `done` (tap the sign, 1.8 s), `hang` (toy to peg, 2.4 s). A touch ends the hand at once. A demo never starts while the camera is still gliding (it waits for the glide), and every hand point is a world point re-projected each frame (engine `fx.ghostPath`), so the hand always lands on the pot, the dish, the sign or the hook.
- **Stuck ladder (no touch):** engine ladder at 5 s (the instruction again as a caption, plus a nudge: the right pot, the dish, the stamp, the sign or the toys bob, or the toy's outline pulses), 8 s (a halo on the target and the hand demo of the needed action), 20 s (errorless help, below), then the engine's go-home. The game also replays the hand every 8 s without the right action.
- **Errorless help (20 s, or the second wrong-colour lift in a step):** directed L2 steps put lids on the wrong pots, leaving the right one open; in the spots step the stamp is put in her hand and the lids go on the wrong pots; free rounds replay the hand and bob the pot; the choose step puts a halo on all three toys and replays the scan (it never chooses for her); the hang step replays the hang demo; in L3 mixing it replays the stir hand (if both paints are in the dish) or lids the pots not in the recipe.
- **Wrong colour in a directed step (L2 and L3 painting):** when her finger lifts after painting the asked part in another colour, the first time in the step: the friend (or Pip if there is no friend) shakes its head with a "nope", its face goes calm for 1.2 s, the step's line ("Duck is yellow!") is said, voiced, and the right pot bobs (in L3 the dish). The second time: the lids go on the wrong pots. The wrong paint is not removed; she paints over it.
- **Pip:** watches from the left (L1, L2) or the back left by the pots (L3), turns a little and nods or flaps once when she hangs a toy.
- **Friends (L2 rounds 1 to 4 and 8, L3 rounds 2 and 6):** hop up onto the table from the meadow at the right end and walk in along a front lane (z −0.45) to the friend spot, starting 0.3 s after any leaving friend has set off along the back edge (z −1.05), so two friends never cross; the hops on and off the table take 1.0 s. When her toy is ready to hang, the friend sees itself in its colour from its own spot (it never walks in front of the toy): a look at the toy, then at the child, joy face, one call (quack, ribbit, oink, cheep) or claw-waving. Friends who sat come back for the finale.
- **Picture card (L2 rounds 5 to 7, L3 rounds 3, 4, 5, 7):** a small stand at the friend spot showing the finished picture; it swaps its picture for the next card round; it is pressed (a small bump) when the toy is ready.
- **Two-part toys:** when a step names a part, that part's outline pulses once (1.3 s). On a "clip" round paint stays inside the part the stroke started on.

## Level 3: make the colour, then paint

The pots are always red, yellow, blue and white; the round's `open` pots are open, the rest lidded. Mixing: dip a pot, touch the dish (a lumpy puddle of that colour spreads on its left); dip a second pot, touch the dish (a puddle on the right); "Stir!" and she rubs round the dish. **The colour follows her rubbing:** the new colour is laid in only as far as her finger travels, and the two parent streaks thin out as she stirs, so the dish looks done when it is done (about one slow circle, 1.7 units of rubbing); rubbing straight on after the second dab already stirs. Tapping the dish also stirs (12 % a tap, so rubbing is quicker). A soft `squish` follows her finger. When the colour is made, two small recipe dots appear on the dish's rim, the recipe is said ("Red and yellow make orange!", voiced once per level), the brush comes up loaded from the dish and the camera glides back to the easel. The dish is now her paint source: when the brush runs dry the dish bobs and the `dip` hand taps the dish. A wrong mix never loads the brush: "Let's make green!" (voiced, the same line as the intro), the dish goes onto the used pile, a fresh dish comes off the spare stack, and the camera stays on the mix view. After 3 wrong mixes the lids go on the pots not in the recipe. Used dishes shrink to saucer size and go onto the one tidy pile behind the spare stack; touching the pile pulses the top dish's recipe dots and says its recipe (how to make that colour again, voiced).
